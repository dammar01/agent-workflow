# Research log — 2026-10-05

A research-period snapshot covering the reframing of `agent-workflow` after comparing its direction with broader agent harnesses, especially Raven. The current status of claims lives in the linked research records, not here.

## The shift in the research question

```text
Before   How can agent-workflow improve coding-agent workflows through delegation,
         context externalization, and orchestration?

After    How much agentic computation should be allocated to a task, and when does
         additional agent decomposition stop producing sufficient marginal value
         relative to its resource and coordination cost?
```

The question is now framed as a resource-allocation problem rather than as a general multi-agent architecture problem.

The primary research question is tracked as RQ-16:

> What degree of agent decomposition is optimal for a task under constraints of token usage, latency, human intervention, and coordination overhead?

Agent scaling is tracked separately as RQ-17.

## The revised research thesis

> `agent-workflow` studies whether additional agentic decomposition produces sufficient marginal task value to justify the additional token, latency, human-intervention, and coordination costs it introduces.

The project therefore does not assume that more agents are better or that fewer agents are better.

The current working hypothesis is diminishing marginal return: increasing agentic decomposition may improve task outcomes initially, but beyond a task-dependent point the additional cost may equal or exceed the additional task value.

The hypothesis remains unvalidated until a controlled experiment varies decomposition while holding the task sufficiently constant.

See [CHARTER.md](../CHARTER.md), [RQ-16](../questions/RQ-16-optimal-agent-decomposition.yaml), and [RQ-17](../questions/RQ-17-agent-scaling.yaml).

## The Raven comparison

Raven was examined as a broader reference for agent composition and harness design.

The comparison established that Raven has a substantially broader abstraction surface, including specialized agents, harness composition, memory, dynamic workers, DAG execution, hooks, and agent evolution.

This does not invalidate `agent-workflow`.

The distinction is:

```text
Raven            How can specialized agents/harnesses be composed
                 into broader agentic capability?

agent-workflow   How much agentic computation/decomposition should
                 be allocated to a task under resource constraints?
```

The important conclusion is that feature breadth and research relevance are different dimensions.

A broader agent framework is not automatically a better vehicle for answering the project's research question.

## The scope boundary

A major risk identified during the comparison was a possible "Raven-lite" direction: adding DAGs, specialist agents, memory systems, evolution, or other general-purpose mechanisms primarily because another agent platform contains them.

This would increase architectural breadth without necessarily producing research evidence.

The resulting decision rule is:

```text
Feature
   ↓
Research Variable
   ↓
Experiment
   ↓
Evidence
```

A new mechanism should therefore identify the research problem it enables, the variable it changes, the experiment it makes possible, the evidence expected from that experiment, its complexity, and whether the existing runtime can already support the same measurement.

This boundary is now recorded in [CHARTER.md](../CHARTER.md) §6–§8.

## The resource model

The research now separates measurement from decision.

The measured resource vector is:

```text
R = (N, T, L, H, K)
```

where:

```text
N = delegated agent executions
T = token/computation
L = latency
H = human intervention
K = coordination overhead
```

These quantities are measured independently because they do not share a common unit.

A total cost exists only at the decision level after an experiment defines normalization and weights:

```text
Cost(N) = wT T_hat(N)
        + wL L_hat(N)
        + wH H_hat(N)
        + wK K_hat(N)
```

The intended decision is therefore:

```text
minimum decision-level cost
subject to acceptable task quality
```

rather than minimizing any one resource independently.

## What the existing real-use data can and cannot show

The real-use benchmark continues to provide useful observational evidence.

Across the existing task data, the observed outcome by delegated-call count was not monotonic: tasks with 3–5 delegated calls had a higher observed solution rate than tasks with 6 or more.

This is useful as motivation for controlled research because it suggests that additional delegation does not automatically imply better outcomes.

It is not evidence that 3–5 calls is optimal.

The number of delegated calls is confounded with task size, task type, and difficulty. Therefore CASE-012 cannot answer RQ-16 or RQ-17 by itself.

The needed experiment must fix the task and deliberately vary the decomposition level.

## The experiment now implied by the research question

The primary experimental direction is a decomposition sweep:

```text
N = 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
```

For the same task, measure:

```text
Quality
Tokens
Latency
Human intervention
Coordination overhead
```

The analysis should focus on marginal return:

```text
ΔQ(N) = Q(N) - Q(N-1)
```

against the corresponding decision-level marginal cost:

```text
ΔC(N) = C(N) - C(N-1)
```

The point of interest is task-dependent:

```text
ΔQ(N) ≤ ΔC(N)
```

rather than a universal number of agents.

## Corrections made during this period

The research documentation was aligned so that there is one primary thesis rather than multiple competing project theses.

Previous real-use observations were explicitly separated from causal claims.

The distinction between raw measurements and weighted decision-level cost was made explicit in the research charter.

The role of context externalization was also narrowed: its relevance is now primarily as a question of **context allocation**, not simply token reduction.

The current research records explicitly state that implementation, observation, and validation are different states.

## Deliberately not recorded

* The claim that Raven's broader feature set means `agent-workflow` should reproduce those features.
* Any claim that 3–5 delegated calls is an optimal range.
* Any universal claim that fewer agents or more agents are inherently better.
* Claims that higher real-use success after delegation is caused by delegation.
* Architectural work justified only by feature parity with another agent framework.
* AI-assisted discussion as research evidence.
* Product strategy claims that are unrelated to the research question.

## Relation to the earlier research direction

The earlier research identified externalized exploration, context management, human orchestration, latency, and persistent knowledge as meaningful mechanisms.

Those observations are retained, but they are now treated as supporting variables or mechanisms within the broader resource-allocation question rather than as separate competing theses.

The change is therefore a **reframing of the research hierarchy**, not a rejection of the previous observations.

## Current position

The research has a defined primary question, resource model, hypothesis, scope boundary, and controlled-experiment direction.

What remains unresolved is empirical:

```text
What does Q(N) actually look like for controlled tasks?

How rapidly do T, L, H, and K increase with decomposition?

At what task-dependent point does additional agentic
computation stop being worth its cost?
```

These questions remain unanswered.
